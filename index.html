02Profile and positioning

Bridging enterprise technology risk assurance with hands-on offensive security.

Most security work looks at a system from one side. Mine looks at it from both: whether the controls would satisfy an auditor, and whether they would stop an attacker.

The auditor's perspective

Are security controls appropriately designed, implemented and operating effectively?

  • Tests of design and operating effectiveness
  • Risk and control matrices, sampling and evidence
  • ISO/IEC 27001, COBIT 2019, COSO and NIST CSF
+

The attacker's perspective

Can an adversary bypass those controls, exploit weaknesses and achieve meaningful business impact?

  • Attack-path analysis within an authorised scope
  • Exploitation, privilege escalation and lateral movement
  • Findings mapped to MITRE ATT&CK techniques

The combined result

Risk ratings grounded in what is actually exploitable, and remediation traced back to the control that failed, so fixes last beyond the next patch cycle.

Illustrative scenarios

A former employee's domain account still signs in, and it still has administrator rights.

Adversary lens

Valid credentials get past most perimeter defences and blend in with normal activity. An orphaned admin account is a quiet route to the whole domain.

Technique
T1078.002 Valid Accounts: Domain Accounts

Assurance lens

Do joiner, mover and leaver controls remove access on termination, and does the periodic privileged access review catch what they miss?

Test of design
Walk through the HR-to-IT leaver workflow and the access review procedure.
Test of operating effectiveness
Sample leavers and compare termination dates with account-disable dates; inspect evidence of completed reviews.

A standard domain user can reach Domain Admin through a service account with a weak password.

Adversary lens

Any authenticated user can request Kerberos service tickets for accounts with SPNs and crack them offline. A privileged service account with a weak password becomes the escalation path.

Technique
T1558.003 Kerberoasting

Assurance lens

Are service accounts inventoried, given strong managed passwords and least privilege, and is privileged activity monitored?

Test of design
Review the service-account standard, password policy and privileged access management coverage.
Test of operating effectiveness
Extract privileged group membership, reconcile it to the approved list and check service-account password age.

An internet-facing service runs a version listed in CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog.

Adversary lens

A public exploit and confirmed in-the-wild use make this the first thing an opportunistic attacker tries. No credentials are needed.

Developers can deploy straight to production without an independent approval.

Adversary lens

Compromise one developer account and malicious code can reach production with nobody else reviewing it.

05IT audit and technology risk

Can management rely on these controls?

Technology risk assurance answers that question for boards, regulators and external auditors. Choose an ITGC domain to trace how a risk becomes an audit conclusion.

ITGC assurance architecture

Illustrative control examples

IT general control domains

In-scope applications, databases, operating systems and infrastructure that financial reporting and critical services depend on

RiskControl objectiveControl activityAudit evidenceTest of designTest of operating effectivenessAudit conclusion
  1. Risk

    IT strategy, policies and accountabilities are undefined or unmonitored, so technology risk is not managed in line with business objectives.

  2. Control objective

    Technology is directed and overseen by management in line with risk appetite.

  3. Control activity

    An IT steering committee approves strategy and policies and reviews IT risks and KPIs at a defined frequency.

  4. Audit evidence

    Committee charter, minutes, approved policy set, IT risk register and action tracker.

  5. Test of design

    Inspect the charter and policies; confirm membership, frequency and escalation routes are adequate.

  6. Test of operating effectiveness

    Sample meetings in the period; confirm they took place, reviewed risk and tracked actions to closure.

  7. Audit conclusion

    Effective, or a deficiency rated for its effect on the control environment (COSO, COBIT EDM and APO).

  1. Risk

    Unauthorised or excessive access to financially significant applications and data.

  2. Control objective

    Access is granted, changed and removed on the basis of approved business need.

  3. Control activity

    Access requests are approved by the line manager and system owner; leavers are disabled within the defined timeframe; privileged access is reviewed periodically.

  4. Audit evidence

    System-generated user lists, HR leaver reports, access tickets and signed-off review records.

  5. Test of design

    Walk through joiner, mover and leaver processes, the review procedure and segregation-of-duties rules.

  6. Test of operating effectiveness

    Sample joiners and leavers; compare termination dates to disable dates; re-perform a privileged access review.

  7. Audit conclusion

    Exceptions rated by severity, compensating controls considered, root cause and owner agreed.

  1. Risk

    Unauthorised or untested changes reach production and corrupt data or processing.

  2. Control objective

    Changes to applications, databases and infrastructure are authorised, tested and approved before deployment.

  3. Control activity

    Changes are logged, tested outside production, approved and deployed by someone other than the developer.

  4. Audit evidence

    System-generated change population, tickets, test results, approvals and deployment logs.

  5. Test of design

    Review the change policy, environment separation and who holds production deployment rights.

  6. Test of operating effectiveness

    Reconcile production changes to approved tickets; sample emergency changes for retrospective approval.

  7. Audit conclusion

    Effective, or a finding on unapproved changes or developer access to production, with impact on data integrity.

  1. Risk

    Batch jobs, interfaces or incidents fail without detection, leaving processing incomplete or inaccurate.

  2. Control objective

    Scheduled processing is monitored and failures are resolved promptly.

  3. Control activity

    The scheduler alerts on failure; operators investigate and resolve failed jobs; incidents are tracked to closure.

  4. Audit evidence

    Job schedules, failure logs, incident tickets and scheduler access lists.

  5. Test of design

    Inspect monitoring configuration and incident procedures; confirm scheduler access is restricted.

  6. Test of operating effectiveness

    Sample failed jobs and incidents; trace each to timely investigation and resolution.

  7. Audit conclusion

    Effective, or a deficiency where failures went unresolved or scheduler access was too broad.

  1. Risk

    Data cannot be restored after corruption, accidental deletion or ransomware.

  2. Control objective

    Critical data is backed up as defined and can be restored within the agreed recovery point.

  3. Control activity

    Automated backups with failure alerts, periodic restore tests, and backups kept apart from production administrator accounts.

  4. Audit evidence

    Backup configuration, job reports, restore-test records and backup console access lists.

  5. Test of design

    Review backup policy, retention, and immutable or offline copies.

  6. Test of operating effectiveness

    Sample backup jobs and restore tests; confirm failures were followed up.

  7. Audit conclusion

    Effective, or a finding on untested restores or backups reachable with domain administrator rights.

  1. Risk

    A disruption causes a prolonged outage of critical services.

  2. Control objective

    Critical services can be recovered within approved recovery time and point objectives.

  3. Control activity

    Business continuity and DR plans based on a business impact analysis, exercised regularly with lessons learned.

  4. Audit evidence

    Business impact analysis, BCP and DR plans, DR test reports and remediation tracker.

  5. Test of design

    Check the plans cover critical systems, roles, dependencies and recovery objectives.

  6. Test of operating effectiveness

    Inspect the latest DR test; compare achieved recovery times with RTO and verify follow-up actions.

  7. Audit conclusion

    Effective, or a finding where recovery was never demonstrated or objectives were missed.

Generic examples of common ITGC practice, written for illustration. They are not taken from any client engagement.

Audit methodology

Plan and scope

Agree objectives with the engagement lead and the client, identify in-scope applications and infrastructure, and focus effort where risk is highest.

Activities
  • Understand the business and IT landscape
  • Identify key systems and data flows
  • Risk-based scoping
Output
  • Scope and audit plan
  • Information request list

Understand and document

Walk through each process with control owners, document how controls actually work, and map risks to the controls that address them.

Activities
  • Walkthroughs and interviews
  • Process and system documentation
  • Risk-to-control mapping
Output
  • Risk and Control Matrix (RCM)
  • Walkthrough working papers

Test of design (ToD)

Decide whether each control, if it operated as described, would actually prevent or detect the risk it is meant to address.

Activities
  • Inspect policies, configurations and procedures
  • Assess precision and frequency
  • Identify design gaps
Output
  • ToD conclusions per control
  • Design deficiencies

Test of operating effectiveness (ToE)

Confirm the control worked consistently throughout the period by testing a sample of real occurrences against evidence.

Activities
  • Sample selection from complete populations
  • Re-performance and inspection
  • System-generated evidence
Output
  • ToE working papers
  • Exceptions and deviations

Report findings

Rate each finding by risk, explain the business impact plainly, and agree practical recommendations with the people who own the fix.

Activities
  • Risk rating and root-cause analysis
  • Management responses and owners
  • Executive summary
Output
  • Management letter
  • Agreed action plan

Follow up

Check that agreed actions were implemented and that remediated controls now operate effectively.

Activities
  • Track action status
  • Retest remediated controls
Output
  • Closure evidence
  • Updated risk position
