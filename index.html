02About

Two disciplines, one view of risk

Most security work looks at a system from one side. My work looks at it from both: how it would be attacked, and whether the controls around it would hold.

I am a Senior IT Auditor at BDO Khaled & Co. (BDO Egypt). Since August 2024 I have led more than 30 ITGC, information security and cybersecurity audits for clients in banking and financial services, energy and petroleum, retail, FMCG and fintech, drawing on more than five years of cybersecurity experience.

On the assurance side, I test the design and operating effectiveness of technology controls, build risk and control matrices and IT risk assessments, and evaluate control maturity across core banking, SAP, Oracle, Microsoft Dynamics 365, Active Directory and Microsoft Entra ID. My work is aligned with ISO/IEC 27001, COBIT 2019, COSO, NIST CSF, MITRE ATT&CK and PCI DSS.

On the offensive side, I practise web, API, network, wireless and Active Directory penetration testing, red team techniques and responsible vulnerability research through public bug bounty programmes on HackerOne. That hands-on work, which places me in the top 1% of TryHackMe users worldwide, shapes how I audit: findings are grounded in how weaknesses are actually exploited, and recommendations are ranked by business impact.

I also build tools. My ISMS Assurance Platform supports ISO/IEC 27001:2022 audit and certification-readiness work end to end, and the SARM Security Arsenal automates reconnaissance, vulnerability analysis and CVE intelligence for my own research.

How I think about the work

  • Evidence over assertion

    A control is effective when testing shows it is, not when a policy says it should be.

  • Think like the adversary, report like the auditor

    Findings start from what is exploitable and end in the language of business risk, owners and deadlines.

  • Fix the root, not the symptom

    Every exploitable weakness is traced back to the control that should have prevented it, so the fix lasts beyond the next patch.

Professional mission

To help organisations see their technology risk the way both an attacker and an auditor would, and to close the gap between controls on paper and controls in practice.

The Dual-Lens method

Three stages, one for each chevron in the SARM mark. Each stage looks through both lenses before moving on.

  1. Assess

    Understand the business, scope the environment and evaluate control design against ISO/IEC 27001, COBIT 2019 and COSO. The output is a gap view ranked by risk.

  2. Validate

    Test how controls perform in practice: operating-effectiveness sampling, vulnerability assessment and authorised penetration testing that shows what an adversary could actually reach.

  3. Assure

    Report findings in business terms, agree remediation with control owners and confirm the fixes hold. Management gets the risk picture; technical teams get what they need to act.

The same weakness, seen twice

Choose a scenario to see how an attacker would use it, how an auditor would test the control that should stop it, and where it maps in the frameworks.

A former employee's domain account still signs in, and it still has administrator rights.

Adversary lens

Valid credentials get past most perimeter defences and blend in with normal activity. An orphaned admin account is a quiet route to the whole domain.

Technique
T1078.002 Valid Accounts: Domain Accounts

Assurance lens

Do joiner, mover and leaver controls remove access on termination, and does the periodic privileged access review catch what they miss?

Test of design
Walk through the HR-to-IT leaver workflow and the access review procedure.
Test of operating effectiveness
Sample leavers and compare termination dates with account-disable dates; inspect evidence of completed reviews.
ISO/IEC 27001:2022
A.5.18A.8.2
COBIT 2019
DSS05.04
ITGC domain
Access to programs and data
Root-cause fix
Link the HR leaver feed to account removal and evidence quarterly privileged access reviews.

A standard domain user can reach Domain Admin through a service account with a weak password.

Adversary lens

Any authenticated user can request Kerberos service tickets for accounts with SPNs and crack them offline. A privileged service account with a weak password becomes the escalation path.

Technique
T1558.003 Kerberoasting

Assurance lens

Are service accounts inventoried, given strong managed passwords and least privilege, and is privileged activity monitored?

Test of design
Review the service-account standard, password policy and privileged access management coverage.
Test of operating effectiveness
Extract privileged group membership, reconcile it to the approved list and check service-account password age.
ISO/IEC 27001:2022
A.8.2A.5.17A.8.16
COBIT 2019
DSS05.04
ITGC domain
Access to programs and data
Root-cause fix
Move service accounts to managed or long random passwords, strip unneeded privilege and alert on unusual ticket requests.

An internet-facing service runs a version listed in CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog.

Adversary lens

A public exploit and confirmed in-the-wild use make this the first thing an opportunistic attacker tries. No credentials are needed.

Technique
T1190 Exploit Public-Facing Application

Assurance lens

Does vulnerability management cover every external asset, rate findings by exploitability and remediate within the defined SLA?

Test of design
Review the vulnerability management policy, asset inventory scope and remediation SLAs.
Test of operating effectiveness
Sample critical findings from scan reports and trace each one to remediation evidence within SLA.
ISO/IEC 27001:2022
A.8.8A.5.9
COBIT 2019
DSS05.07
ITGC domain
IT operations
Root-cause fix
Feed the asset inventory into scan scope, prioritise by KEV listing and exploitability, and report SLA compliance to management.

Developers can deploy straight to production without an independent approval.

Adversary lens

Compromise one developer account and malicious code can reach production with nobody else reviewing it.

Technique
T1078 Valid Accounts, used to abuse deployment rights

Assurance lens

Are changes authorised, tested and deployed by someone other than the developer, with development, test and production kept apart?

Test of design
Review the change management policy and who holds production deployment rights.
Test of operating effectiveness
Reconcile a system-generated list of production changes to approved tickets for the period.
ISO/IEC 27001:2022
A.8.32A.8.31A.5.3
COBIT 2019
BAI06.01
ITGC domain
Program changes
Root-cause fix
Remove developer access to production, enforce approval gates in the pipeline and monitor for unapproved changes.

Backups run every night, but nobody has restored from them, and the backup console uses domain admin credentials.

Adversary lens

With domain admin rights the attacker deletes or encrypts the backups first, then deploys ransomware, leaving nothing to recover from.

Techniques
T1490 Inhibit System Recovery; T1486 Data Encrypted for Impact

Assurance lens

Are backups isolated from production administrator accounts, and are restores tested against agreed recovery objectives?

Test of design
Review the backup and disaster recovery policy, RPO and RTO, and backup access rights.
Test of operating effectiveness
Sample backup jobs and restore tests; inspect the results of the last DR exercise.
ISO/IEC 27001:2022
A.8.13A.5.30
COBIT 2019
DSS04.07
ITGC domain
IT operations
Root-cause fix
Keep immutable or offline copies, separate backup administration credentials and schedule restore tests.

Audit methodology

Plan and scope

Agree objectives with the engagement lead and the client, identify in-scope applications and infrastructure, and focus effort where risk is highest.

Activities
  • Understand the business and IT landscape
  • Identify key systems and data flows
  • Risk-based scoping
Output
  • Scope and audit plan
  • Information request list

Understand and document

Walk through each process with control owners, document how controls actually work, and map risks to the controls that address them.

Activities
  • Walkthroughs and interviews
  • Process and system documentation
  • Risk-to-control mapping
Output
  • Risk and Control Matrix (RCM)
  • Walkthrough working papers

Test of design (ToD)

Decide whether each control, if it operated as described, would actually prevent or detect the risk it is meant to address.

Activities
  • Inspect policies, configurations and procedures
  • Assess precision and frequency
  • Identify design gaps
Output
  • ToD conclusions per control
  • Design deficiencies

Test of operating effectiveness (ToE)

Confirm the control worked consistently throughout the period by testing a sample of real occurrences against evidence.

Activities
  • Sample selection from complete populations
  • Re-performance and inspection
  • System-generated evidence
Output
  • ToE working papers
  • Exceptions and deviations

Report findings

Rate each finding by risk, explain the business impact plainly, and agree practical recommendations with the people who own the fix.

Activities
  • Risk rating and root-cause analysis
  • Management responses and owners
  • Executive summary
Output
  • Management letter
  • Agreed action plan

Follow up

Check that agreed actions were implemented and that remediated controls now operate effectively.

Activities
  • Track action status
  • Retest remediated controls
Output
  • Closure evidence
  • Updated risk position
